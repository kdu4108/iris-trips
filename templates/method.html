How the festival ranking works

This page explains how individual rankings become one ranking for the choir. It uses the same method as our repertoire vote, applied to just 5 options. You don't need any of this to vote. It's here for anyone curious, and for transparency.

1. What your ballot says

Your ballot is the festivals you placed, in order (1 = top choice). Please rank all five. Your #2–#5 matter because they become our plans B to E if a festival doesn't accept us. A festival you leave unranked counts as “no opinion”: it gets no score from your ballot, positive or negative.

2. How a ballot becomes numbers

Each festival you placed gets a score between 0 and 1, based on its position and on how many festivals you placed (n):

score = 1 − (position − ½) / n

With all 5 ranked, that is 0.9, 0.7, 0.5, 0.3, 0.1 for places 1–5: evenly spaced, so moving a festival up one place always counts the same. The colors on your ballot show this live: blue for your upper half, red for your lower half.

3. How ballots are combined

A festival's raw score is the average of its scores over the ballots that placed it. This is a Borda count, rescaled to 0–1. If everyone ranks everything, a higher score simply means a better average position.

If a festival was left unranked by many people, it would rest on fewer opinions. To correct for that, the ordering uses an adjusted score: the raw average blended with 3 neutral phantom ballots at 0.5. When nearly everyone ranks all five, this changes almost nothing.

Two cross-checks appear alongside:

  • #1 votes: how many people picked the festival as their top choice. Averages favor options everyone finds fine. This column shows the options a group is excited about.
  • Head-to-head: for every pair of festivals, how many voters preferred each one. This answers the plan-B question directly: “if A falls through, do people prefer B or C?” A festival that beats all others head-to-head is called the Condorcet winner.

When two scores are closer than one voter swapping two adjacent festivals could change them, they're flagged as statistically tied. The organizers then decide between them, using the head-to-head count among other things.

4. From ranking to trip

The final ranking is the order we apply in. Whether a festival works out also depends on things a vote can't settle: whether the festival selects us, registration deadlines, and the budget. Iris currently expects to cover about {{ iris_chf }} CHF per person.

5. Fairness details

  • Unranked festivals are listed in a random order unique to you, so no festival benefits from always appearing first.
  • Your ballot saves on every change and is counted exactly as last saved when voting closes.
  • Visibility: the organizing team can see individual ballots (by name) during voting. After voting closes, the aggregate results and individual rankings are shared with all members.
